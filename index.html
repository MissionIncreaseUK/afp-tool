Annual Fundraising Plan Tool

Seven steps to a written plan you will actually use, rooted in Mi's Transformational Giving approach.


 

The Annual Fundraising Plan. Work through the seven steps, then the final step compiles your printable plan. Values you enter early carry forward automatically, and cells that fill in for you are shaded green.

Annual Fundraising Plan Template

This tool helps you gather data to set informed goals and plan the steps you will take to meet them. It contains seven steps:

  1. Review Vision & Mission
  2. Assess Current Fundraising Systems
  3. Review Past Fundraising Data
  4. Set Strategic Fundraising Goals
  5. Set Financial Targets
  6. Create Action Steps
  7. Establish an Ongoing Review Process

The final step compiles everything into one printable document to share with your trustees, in a meeting with a champion, or for a trust or foundation application.

Some cells auto fill from earlier steps and are shaded green. Give yourself time for each step, and before you begin each one, pause and pray. We strongly encourage you to work with your Mi UK Area Director while filling in this tool.

Step 1: Review Vision & Mission?An annual plan moves you from reacting to every funding gap to executing on purpose. Grounding it in your vision and mission first keeps the plan connected to why you raise money, not just how much.

Part 1: Where are we headed?

Fundraising is not separate from your mission, it exists to advance it. That's why this plan begins here: prayerfully review your organisational foundation, discerning together with the Lord where you are headed and where you need to remain faithful.

Part 2: What are our financial needs this year?

Prayerfully review your budget. Based on where the Lord is calling you this next year, what do you need?

Part 3: What will affect our fundraising this year?

Prayerfully consider what adjustments or changes need to be made this year. Bring before the Lord the factors that could impact your fundraising this year.

Example: a new Head of Fundraising, rising energy and supply costs, changes to Gift Aid or tax rules affecting major givers.

Consider: What are you adding? How many will be served? How much will it cost? How will it be funded? If ending or changing, consider how many people will be affected and how many champions are invested in those programmes.

Step 2: Assess Current Fundraising Systems?Before you can plan where you're going, you need an honest picture of where you are. Rate each activity as it actually is today, not how you wish it were. That's what sets up every goal in the steps ahead.

A fundraising system is more than one activity in isolation, it's a coordinated set of recommended practices working together toward one purpose: growing champions and resources.

1. Review the list of fundraising systems. Underneath each system are lists of recommended activities. 2. Give a gut-check rating to each of the activities indicating how well your organisation is accomplishing each one. The chart is colour-coded for ease of review. 3. Invite others to rate the following activities to provide multiple perspectives. 4. Review your activity looking for patterns of things that are going well or need attention. 5. Your ratings here will directly shape the goals you set in Step 4 and the action steps you build in Step 6. 6. It's okay if you're not doing all of these yet. Don't expect to score high on everything. This isn't a scorecard, it's a way for you to see the entire picture and help you determine what to focus on next.

Step 4: Set Strategic Fundraising Goals?Choose 1-3 goals about champion growth or process change, not just financial figures. The best goals are honest about where you are and genuinely ambitious about what's possible: neither a wish nor a ceiling.

Strategic fundraising goals are the priorities you will give special time and attention to this year, which is why it is difficult for organisations to focus on more than three at a time. These goals are primarily about the growth of your champions or changes to your ministry's fundraising processes, not just financial figures. Some strategic goals may have no financial figure attached, but rather a champion or process metric. When a change in income is associated with a goal, it is a result of the growth you are pursuing, not the goal itself. If you have identified more than three strategic goals, prioritise them by impact or urgency, and plan to address some of them later.
Write down 1-3 strategic goals you want to accomplish over the next 3-5 years. Articulate them using the SMART categories.

SMART: Specific (What? Who? Why? When? Where?) · Measurable (Can it be evaluated?) · Achievable (Why and how is it attainable and realistic?) · Relevant (How does this grow your impact?) · Time-Bound (When will it be accomplished?)

If a Strategic Goal includes a Change in Income Associated, make sure that change is reflected in the Next Year Target for the appropriate income stream. 3. This analysis may lead you to adjust the goal. 5. Note any anomalies or trends you identified in step 3 that need to be factored in.

Step 6: Create Action Steps?This is where goals become a real calendar and real ownership. Map campaigns and stewardship touchpoints month by month, and assign each action step to a specific owner. Fundraising works best as shared ownership across your team and trustees, not one person's job.

1. Determine the action steps you (and your team) will take in order to reach your strategic fundraising goals and financial targets. List your major action steps on each line and add additional lines as needed. 2. If a strategic goal maps to a specific income stream, place its action steps in that stream's block below. If a goal is focused on champion growth or an internal process that doesn't map to one income stream, use the Goal-Focused Action Steps block instead. 3. Articulate who is ultimately accountable for ensuring each action step is accomplished, and include who else would be involved. Note when you intend to start and when it should be completed. Some action steps are one-and-done with a clear end date, while others are ongoing and should be reviewed on a regular cadence, such as weekly or monthly, rather than closed out. 4. Regularly review this document and update the progress and adjustments columns. 5. Note the last date reviewed.

Step 7: Establish an Ongoing Review Process?A plan only creates value if someone keeps reviewing it. Build a quarterly rhythm around four questions: where are we against each metric, what worked and why, what didn't and what needs adjusting, and what's next.

1. A fundraising plan only creates value if it gets used. Name who will lead the ongoing review process and who else will be involved. 2. Set a meeting frequency and hold to it. This plan is a living document meant to be reviewed every 30-60 days, not filed away and revisited once a year. 3. Build prayer into your review rhythm alongside the financial and organisational review, not as an afterthought. 4. Establish how changes and recommendations to the plan get approved, so adjustments can happen as needed rather than waiting for the next annual planning cycle. 5. Identify the specific reports your team needs, how often each is produced, and who is responsible for reviewing them. 6. Structure each review around four questions: Where are we against each metric? What worked, and why? What didn't, and what needs to change? What are our priorities for the next quarter?

Saving and Resuming Your Work

Nothing you type here is sent anywhere or saved automatically. Use the buttons above to keep your progress:

  1. Save (CSV / Excel) downloads everything you've entered as one file, named for your ministry and today's date. It opens in Excel, Google Sheets, or Numbers if you ever want to look at the raw data, but you don't need to open it. Just keep the file somewhere you'll find it again, like a shared drive or your desktop.
  2. Import loads a previously saved file back into this tool, exactly where you left off. Click Import, choose the CSV file you saved earlier, and every field will fill back in.
  3. Print draft plan exports the finished, formatted plan from the Write Your Plan tab. Use this once your plan is complete and ready to share.
  4. Print all steps prints every step as a worksheet, with blank boxes for anything you haven't filled in yet, so a team can work through it on paper. In the print window, choose Save as PDF if you want a digital copy.

Save often, especially before closing this page. If you're working on this with your Area Director or team, save a copy after each working session so everyone has the latest version.
